Target. Exact when Kalshi can be reached (through a relay). Otherwise it is estimated from the page's own prices over the minute before the open, and the exchanges here sit a few dollars from the official index. Enter the exact target from Robinhood for this window. It can also estimate the exchange-to-index offset when the opening minute is complete. That offset can change; reconfirm the target each window. Buy signals wait for a retrieved or entered target.

      
Sync. Another way to measure the same difference: type Robinhood's big number while it is showing.

      
Closing average, projected. The contract settles on the average of the last 60 seconds. Until that minute starts none of those seconds has happened, and a random walk's best forecast for every one of them is the price now, so the projection sits on spot (plus the lean) and only its uncertainty shrinks as the close nears. In the final minute, available exchange-proxy samples contribute to the projection and future samples follow spot. Missing elapsed samples pause the model. Reconstructed Coinbase samples are identified. Only Kalshi confirms the winning outcome.

      
Timing. A trailing 60-second average smooths price changes. Its effective delay depends on the price path and is not a measured network delay or a guaranteed trading lead. The fast line here is exchange consensus; the blue line is its complete trailing average. Neither is the official BRTI tick stream.

      
Relay. Browsers are not allowed to read Kalshi directly. A small relay of your own (a free Cloudflare Worker; the code is in the README) lets this page read the exact target, the official 60-second average and Kalshi's own Up price. Paste its address here once.

      
Fees. The edge figures take off Robinhood's commission and Kalshi's 1¢ fee, about 2¢ a contract in the middle of the range.

      
Buy, thin, no. The chance is shown with a band: the range it covers if volatility were 30% lower or 50% higher, if the lean were off, and if spot moved one standard deviation of a ten-second move against you while you placed the order. Buy means the price still leaves 3¢ after fees at the bad end of that band, and comes with a size: a quarter of the Kelly stake on the bad end, never more than 2% of the bankroll set below. Thin means there is an edge at the model's own chance but not at the bad end. No means none at the model's chance.

      
Record. At 10, 5, 2 and 1 minutes before each close the page notes its chance and Kalshi's price, and only after Kalshi confirms the outcome it scores both (a Brier score: the squared miss, so 0 is perfect and 0.25 is a coin flip). It also scores every buy call as one contract held to settlement. Until the model's score is below the market's over a few dozen windows, treat buy calls as a hypothesis, not an edge.

      
Sharp moves. Sudden jumps can invalidate the forecast. The model uses a fat-tailed curve, with displayed probabilities bounded to 1–99%; that does not prove calibration. The "Sharp move" note appears when spot moves several estimated standard deviations in ten seconds. The projection assumes its remaining price forecast holds.

      
Order flow. Every trade on the five index exchanges and the Hyperliquid perpetual is sorted by who crossed the spread: a market buy counts as buying, a market sell as selling. The bars are the difference, in dollars, and the line is the running total since the open. A move with heavy flow behind it tends to carry on; a move on balanced flow is more often a sweep that fades. Prints of $200k or more are listed under the chart.

      
Flow lean. The page also measures, as it runs, how far spot has tended to move in the 30 seconds after a stretch of net buying or selling. Once that relation is clear (about ten minutes of data at the least, usually more) the projection leans the way the flow points, by that measured amount and never by more than two standard deviations of a 30-second move. The flow line says whether the lean is on. It is forgotten slowly, so what it learnt this evening still counts tomorrow morning. Liquidations come from Bybit, OKX, Deribit, BitMEX and Binance (Binance refuses US connections): longs being liquidated is forced selling, shorts being liquidated is forced buying, and a run of them is what makes a move overshoot.

      
Sound. Tap Sound once (browsers will not play anything until you do). Then: two rising notes for a sharp move up, two falling for down; three notes when Kalshi's own price leaves a 3¢ edge for Up or Down; one low note at one minute to go. On an iPhone the ring/silent switch must be on ring.

      
The chance shown is a model: a random walk from spot with recently measured volatility and fat tails. Flow and the perp gap may enter the fitted lean; liquidations are shown as context. This exchange-based probability is uncalibrated and is not the official contract outcome.
